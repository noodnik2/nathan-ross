April 4, 1950

Dear Nathan,

It’s many weeks past since I received your last letter. I was quite busy for the last month before our Passover,
to distribute Palestine wine to our Jewish members as the Government would not let me sell this in the Gov't Store,
and there was some work to be done in the house, as a few of our rooms got empty, and before we may rent it I have to
paint it and put new linoleum. And so time passed, and now after we have celebrated the Seder night together with all
our family, we have in our house 22 including the children of the Pelmans, Aqua's (Sid came from Seattle). Mama
has done lots of work, with Mallie. And so tonight, I thought, I will drop you a line or two, to go back to your
letter.  

I really don’t know what to say. I think you know yourself how we should feel, you have said it in your letter.
We are wrong in your way, you may be right, but we are not wrong, too. I want you to understand, the way Mama and I
have been brought up in my Hebrew School, the teaching is different from the School teaching in this country.  The
Jewish books teach How to live, not how to earn a living; here they teach how to make money.  Schools for Dr.'s,
lawyers, musicians, business college and so on. It is all how to make more money, there is no teaching how to respect
each other, or how to respect your older brother, your father and mother. I can’t write this all as there are books
of 1,000s of pages. And all is about how a man should live and be happy. I will just give you one story—not a Robin
Hood story.

There was a great teacher, Rabbi Akiva, he had 400 students. R. Akiva had one child, which he loved very much.
One day his child became sick, and the Dr. gave up all hope of its recovery. So R.A. left the child with the Dr.
and nurse, he himself went to the Synagogue. And \[after] 3 days in succession \[when he] did not eat or drink
\[and] has prayed to god, he was so weak he could not stay on his feet, but still refused to eat or drink. On the 4th
day the child was dead. All his students came together to the synagogue, seeing their Rabbi and teacher is weak and pale,
they was afraid to tell him about his child’s passing away.  As the R.A. noticed them, he called to them and said “Tell
me all what happened,” they could not open their mouth then he asked again, “Is my child dead?” They shook their heads
yes, and watched, expecting he may faint. But instead he called on them and said, “Bring me to the holy ark.” When he
reached the ark, he called out, “O Lord, You are always right. Your judgment is right. You have given to me and You
have taken back from me.” Then he said to his students, “Bring to me something and I will eat.” All the students were
dumbfounded and could not open their mouths. Rabbi Akiva said to them, “Don’t you understand? When my child was
alive, I was praying to God, and now when all is over, I have no more to say. And it is God’s will; I have to make the
best of it.”

It may be just a story, but we can learn the way how to live in this world. I think my son, you will understand us,
and not to have in your imagination, that I don’t want to see you happy, but just to please myself. Yes, I would
be happy, like all the rest of my friends, to celebrate but one day in my life at my child’s wedding.  Once in my
life to see my children’s family dear to me. You have seen how dear to me are Ruby, Toby, and their children, and how
much pleasure Mama and myself are getting when Dora and Eddie are coming here with our loving Lauren.  And so I am
looking forwards for your future. You are not less to my heart as the rest of my children, perhaps more. And I fare
if I loss \[fear losing?] all my hopes and dreams.

I want you to know that I am not a fanatic \[about] religion. I do not think that we are better than others. But I
will admit I am a nationalist. But I am not a Hater of other nationals. But it is a question: is the other nationalist
not a hater? There are a few young couples that we know; some are very nice, Jessie and Davies (you know them).
Jessie is more with Davies French, but some of them are in different directions. And you understand that it is a
father and mother’s heart breaking when they have their child to go away from them, being unable to come close to them.

And all what I can say now is I wish both of you all the luck in the world. As for you, you'll be to my heart as
long as I live. And if your loving wife will love my son, I will admire her like the rest of my family. Your letters
to us will be my best wish, and again with happiness to both.

Your loving dad
